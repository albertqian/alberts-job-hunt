# Albert's Writing Style Guide (for LinkedIn post drafts)

Derived from Albert's published blog writing (see `writing_samples/`). Add recent LinkedIn
posts to `writing_samples/` as .md files to sharpen the voice; the agent reads every file there.

## Voice patterns to reproduce
1. **Open with a familiar, everyday scenario, often "then vs. now."** Example: booking a vacation
   through a travel agent two decades ago vs. planning it on your phone today. The business point
   arrives only after the reader recognizes their own experience.
2. **Name the idea and credit its source.** "...a term coined by Uri Sarid of MuleSoft." Concepts,
   data and quotes are attributed to the person or outlet they came from.
3. **Explain with concrete, recognizable examples.** Healthcare scheduling, banking apps, Amazon/AWS.
   One or two examples, each explained in a sentence or two, rather than a list of logos.
4. **Bridge from consumer experience to enterprise implication.** Move from "what you notice" to
   "what organizations are doing about it" to "why it matters competitively."
5. **Plain, conversational-professional sentences.** Addresses the reader as "you." Light on jargon;
   when a term is needed, define it in the same sentence.
6. **Measured, not hyped.** Uses hedges like "likely" and "presumably." Lets the logic carry the
   argument instead of superlatives. No "game-changer," "revolutionary," "unleash," "in today's
   fast-paced world."
7. **Closes on a reasoned conclusion**, not a slogan.

## LinkedIn adaptation rules
- 150–250 words. Short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences. First line is the hook (the everyday scenario
  or a sharp observation from the news), and it must make sense without "see more."
- Ground the post in ONE real news item from this week; credit the outlet and include its link.
- Point of view: balanced on AI. AI is where the industry is going, but organizations should be
  deliberate about where and how it takes over decisions; keep a human in the loop.
- End with one genuine question to the reader.
- Maximum 3 hashtags at the end; no emoji walls (zero or one emoji).
- Thought leadership only: no job-search or "open to work" signals, no mention of his current
  employer or its products, no confidential or internal information.
